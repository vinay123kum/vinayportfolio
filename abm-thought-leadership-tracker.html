The Problem
Marketing could see the accounts warming up.
Outreach couldn't act on it.

Campaign reporting was a weekly export nobody could ask a follow-up question of. Sales outreach ran from a separate contact sheet. Nothing showed which target accounts had warmed up, who to contact there, or who had already been approached.

So the question wasn't "how is the campaign doing?" It was "which accounts should we talk to next, and through whom?"

The Build
Two teams' data.
One working view.

The platform combines LinkedIn engagement with the target account list, the contact database and the connection log. Marketing sees account movement. Outreach gets a ranked list of who to contact next.

How It Connects
From an ad click to a named follow-up.
One shared account list

The target list lives in Google Sheets and is re-read on every refresh, so new accounts appear without a code change.

One definition of engaged

Both teams read the same tiers, and only on-list companies count, so off-target engagement can't inflate the result.

A real marketing to outreach handoff

Clicked accounts are matched to a person-level contact database, producing 181 named contacts ranked by account clicks, impressions and seniority.

No double outreach

Anyone in the connection request log is excluded by LinkedIn profile, and contacted accounts leave the "reach next" queue.

Change tracking

Dated snapshots show new companies and new clicks since the last run, so the team starts with what moved.

Clicked
Any click. The strongest signal, and the only tier that creates named follow-ups.
Warm
8+ impressions with no click. Counted as engaged and queued behind clicked accounts.
Light
3–7 impressions. Tracked but not counted as engaged.
