Frequently Asked Questions
Why do 12 articles on "least privilege" — including 2 near-duplicate slugs — hurt Veza? +
Google allocates ranking authority to domains, not pages. When 12 Veza pages target the same query — two of them nearly identical slugs — Google splits its crawl attention and link equity across all of them, and the near-duplicate pair actively signals crawl confusion. None of the 12 accumulates enough authority to rank #1. A single consolidated hub page receives 100% of the signal and has a far better shot at dominating the SERP for one of the most fundamental queries in identity security.
What happens to the traffic on the pages that get redirected? +
A 301 redirect passes approximately 90–99% of the original page's link equity to the destination. You don't lose the traffic — you concentrate it. The destination hub page gets stronger, the redirected pages no longer split authority, and Google quickly recrawls and reassigns rankings to the canonical winner.
How fast can Veza move from a 15/100 to a 50+ AI visibility score? +
The technical fixes (301 redirects on the duplicate slugs, noindexing product-update changelogs and PR pages) can be done in a week and Google typically processes them within 2–4 weeks. Hub page consolidations — like the NHI and ISPM-vs-IGA pages — start showing ranking improvements within 6–10 weeks. AI visibility improvements typically follow 6–12 weeks after consolidation as LLMs recrawl and re-rank citations. A full content health upgrade typically takes one quarter to reflect meaningfully in both search rankings and AI visibility scores.
