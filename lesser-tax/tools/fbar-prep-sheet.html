1. The year and the rate

FBAR uses one rate for the whole year: the US Treasury's rate for 31 December of that year. Not the rate on the day your balance peaked, and not your bank's rate.

2. Your accounts

One row per account. Use the highest balance it reached at any point in the year, not the closing balance.

Which accounts count?
  • NRE, NRO and FCNR accounts — yes, including NRE even though it's tax-free in India
  • Savings accounts and fixed deposits — yes
  • PPF — yes
  • Demat accounts and Indian mutual funds — yes
  • ULIPs and life insurance with a cash value — yes
  • Joint accounts with parents — yes, and you report the full balance, not your share
  • Accounts you can sign on but don't own — yes, reported differently (signature authority)
  • Indian real estate held directly — no, property itself isn't a financial account
